THE RIGHT FLOOR FOR THE ROOM

Material matters.

01

Solid Wood

One piece of timber. Can be refinished many times. Moves with humidity; unsuitable for bathrooms or underfloor heating.

02

Engineered Wood

Real wood over a stable core. Often suitable for underfloor heating, subject to product specification. Sanding depends on wear-layer depth.

03

LVT

Resilient and warm underfoot. A practical kitchen or utility choice with correctly prepared subfloor and specified water resistance.

04

Laminate

A useful budget option for rentals or spare rooms. Damaged boards need replacement; water resistance varies by product.

05

Parquet & Herringbone

Blocks laid in a pattern. More fitting labour than planks, reflected openly in the fitting price.

06

Carpet

Wool and wool-blend comfort for bedrooms and stairs. A softer choice where water resistance is not the priority.

SUBFLOOR HONESTY

Why a quote can change
once the old floor comes up.

Nobody knows everything under an existing floor. Cracked screed, rotten board edges and hidden level differences can appear after uplift. Moisture readings and level checks catch much of it, but not every problem.

The sensible process is to stop, show you the issue and quote the additional work before continuing. Levelling compound at £18/m² is a common extra; it belongs on the estimate, not buried in an invoice.

MADE TO LIVE WITH

Care, not guesswork.

The concept proposes 25-year wood, 20-year LVT and 10-year laminate wear cover for normal domestic use. These are illustrative terms, not an issued warranty.

What does wear cover mean?

Wear-layer failure under normal domestic use. Confirm the actual product terms, exclusions and claim process before buying.

How should I clean the floor?

Vacuum grit, use a well-wrung mop and the manufacturer's approved cleaner. Avoid steam mops on wood and laminate.

What is usually excluded?

Water damage, pet claws, dragged furniture and unsuitable cleaning methods. Use furniture pads and clean spills promptly.
