THE CUSTOMER PROBLEM

What if getting started
meant getting something done?

In this fictional team-workspace brief, a new coordinator faces a blank project before understanding the product. The hypothesis: a guided first task is more useful than a longer setup tour.

The decision: hold the launch, define a pilot.

Do not turn an attractive hypothesis into an assumed customer need. A small, consented pilot would test task completion and comprehension before broader release. No research or pilot has actually run.

Research plan

Observe a small range of first-time coordinators completing one real task, ask where confidence drops and review consented onboarding events. Interview count and findings are deliberately not invented.

Scope & cuts

Proposed MVP: one guided task, a visible completion state and a skip option. Defer AI setup, templates marketplace and multi-team administration until the core path is understood.

Opportunity sizing / assumption sandbox

Synthetic monthly eligible teams × hypothetical activation lift. This is potential extra activated teams, not revenue or a forecast.

Proposed gates: consented instrumentation, clear task completion definition, accessible empty/error states, support ownership and an agreed rollback path. Risks include confusing “tour finished” with first value, selection bias in recruited teams and onboarding data quality.

Local exercise: hold. Customer evidence and rollout gates are not yet established.

02 / ROADMAP CASE
Question: what next?
Framework: RICE

Make the disagreement
specific enough to discuss.

Sales wants export, support wants saved replies, and platform engineering wants an access audit. The worksheet makes a synthetic ranking visible; it does not replace strategy, safety obligations or stakeholder judgment.

RICE / editable effort assumptions

Score = reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort. Reach: people/month; impact: illustrative relative score; confidence: fraction; effort: person-months. All values are invented for this exercise.

RequestReachImpactConfidenceEffortScore

Stakeholders & tradeoff

Proposed discussion: sales brings customer context, support brings workflow friction, engineering brings dependencies, and a product lead owns the decision. RICE cannot turn weak estimates into reliable evidence.

Delivery risk

Exports may expose permission mistakes. Saved replies can become stale. An access audit may be a prerequisite even if its score ranks lower. Treat security and compliance obligations as constraints, not optional score competitors.

Inspect a proposed sequence, not a promise +

First: establish access requirements and dependencies. Next: test saved replies with support. Later: scope export with permission-safe defaults. Revisit estimates when the team learns; no dates or real stakeholders are asserted.

What cannot be attributed to the launch? +

Everything causal: there was no real launch or randomized experiment. In a real before/after comparison, acquisition mix, seasonality, support intervention and missing events could change the rate. Define eligibility, exclusions, cohort timing and guardrails before interpreting any movement.
