01 / THE BRIEF

Make the last step
the easiest one.

TechFlow is a fictional consumer technology retailer. The sample brief: rethink the product-to-checkout journey for shoppers who want clarity, not another account.

Deliverables: journey map, wireframes, visual system, responsive checkout concept and handoff notes. Proposed tools: Figma and React; this actual presentation uses standalone HTML/CSS/JavaScript.

02 / DISCOVERY

The proposed research plan combines interviews, a competitor audit and funnel analytics. No real interviews or analytics were collected for this fictional study.

Unexpected costs

Hypothesis: forced sign-up interrupts purchase intent.

Unclear progress

Hypothesis: shoppers need a predictable next step.

Journey: Discover → Compare → Add to bag → Understand costs → Review → Confirm.

05 / REFLECTION

Clarity is a feature.

The strongest idea is a smaller decision surface: explain cost early, offer guest checkout and keep each action legible. In a real project, I would test comprehension before polishing visual details, then measure accessibility and funnel behavior after launch.

Next iteration: include payment failure, address correction and assistive-technology testing. No awards or press coverage are claimed.
