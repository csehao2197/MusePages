01 / ANALYSIS
Question → evidence
12 synthetic weeks
No missing observations

Volume is a clue.
It is not the whole story.

Question: do response hours rise alongside ticket volume? The illustrative series rises overall, with week-to-week variation. This association cannot establish why response time changed.

Complete synthetic dataset · no hidden rows
WeekTickets (count)Response (hours)Split

Provenance & cleaning

Hand-authored synthetic values for this page, not sampled from people or an organization. All 12 rows have integer week and ticket counts, positive response hours and no duplicates. No imputation or exclusion is needed.

Assumptions & limits

Equal-length weeks, consistent response-time definition and comparable queues are assumed. Staffing, complexity and seasonality are absent. Twelve rows are insufficient for a decision-grade forecast.

02 / MODEL CARD
Baseline → evaluation
Train: weeks 1–9
Holdout: weeks 10–12

A better score is only
the start of the question.

The baseline predicts the training mean. A least-squares line uses week number as its only feature. Both are fit on weeks 1–9 only, with the last three weeks held out in time order. There is no separate tuning set or hyperparameter search in this tiny example.

Mean baseline MAE · hours
Linear trend MAE · hours

Computed from the embedded synthetic rows in your browser. MAE = mean absolute prediction error on weeks 10–12.

Leakage prevention

Holdout values are not used in fitting or selecting the model. The only predictor is week index, known in advance. A real study needs a predeclared evaluation protocol and rolling validation.

Failure analysis

A straight line cannot adapt to a staffing change, queue spike or seasonal break. Low error on three orderly synthetic weeks is not evidence of production reliability. No deployment or real-world improvement is claimed.
