LESSON DESIGN · CURATED STARTING POINT
# Understanding equivalent fractions
Grade 4 · Mathematics · Advanced
Curriculum: School curriculum · review required
3 sessions × 60 minutes · Focus: Whole-child learning

Curated, deterministic teaching template. Adapt to your class and verify local curriculum alignment.

## 01 / Know your learners
### Prior knowledge
Recognise numerator and denominator; identify halves and quarters of equal-sized wholes.
### Likely learning barrier
A larger denominator can look like a larger quantity. Always compare equal-sized wholes.
### A bridge to understanding
Fold equal paper strips into halves, quarters and eighths. Align the strips before writing symbols.
Require multiple representations, critique assumptions and add an independent cross-context investigation.

## 02 / Learning with intention
### Knowledge & skills
- Apply the core ideas to a practical situation, give one reasoned explanation and contribute one respectful peer-feedback comment.
- Identify and correct one common misconception using a worked example.
### Process & method
- Compare two approaches and explain one choice to a partner.
- Use an exit response to identify a specific next step.
### Attitudes & values
- Listen respectfully and give one constructive comment on a peer's reasoning.

## 03 / Teaching priorities & preparation
Key learning: Equivalent fractions name the same amount. Multiply or divide numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number.
Challenge: A larger denominator can look like a larger quantity. Always compare equal-sized wholes.
Teacher preparation: Equal-length paper strips, coloured pencils, fraction grids and a teacher-prepared number line.
Student preparation: bring a notebook and recall one relevant idea; no paid materials required.
Teaching approach: guided inquiry, paired discussion and gradual release.

## 04 / Session 1: Discover & understand
60 minutes · Advanced group
### Connect / 5 minutes
Purpose: activate prior knowledge and surface a misconception.
Activity: Show one half-strip and two quarter-strips. Ask: Is one piece always less than two pieces?
Expected response: pupils share a prediction; listen for the barrier described above.
Teacher says: “What makes you think that? Show me your evidence.”
### Explore / 22 minutes
Purpose: build a shared model.
Activity: Fold and align paper strips. Label 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8. Model multiplying both parts by 2, then ask pupils to explain why the covered length stays constant.
Expected response: pupils explain a step, not just an answer; pause if fewer than four out of five sampled responses are secure.
Teacher says: “Which part changes? Which idea still holds? Explain it to your partner.”
### Practise / 18 minutes
Purpose: gather individual evidence and offer appropriate challenge.
- Foundation / everyone: Draw and label two models showing 1/2 = 2/4. Use equal-sized wholes.
- Improve / optional: Find the missing numerator: 3/5 = ?/10. Explain the scale factor with a diagram.
- Extend / challenge: Compare 2/3 and 4/6 using two representations. Explain why 2/3 does not equal 2/6.
Teacher says: “Start where you can explain your thinking, then stretch one step further.”
Support: Require multiple representations, critique assumptions and add an independent cross-context investigation.
Expected response: an annotated model or written justification. Re-model for the support group; extend pupils who demonstrate secure understanding.
### Transfer / 10 minutes
Purpose: test understanding beyond the modelled example.
Activity: A recipe uses 3/4 cup of milk. Which eighth-cup measure gives the same amount? Justify.
Expected response: pupils connect the new situation to a key idea and justify their approach.
Teacher says: “How do you know your explanation works in this new situation?”
### Reflect & next steps / 5 minutes
Purpose: check the learning goal and set an appropriate follow-up.
Exit ticket: Complete 2/3 = ?/6 and explain the operation on both parts.
Reference / criteria: 4/6; multiply numerator and denominator by 2. Equal-sized wholes are essential.
Teacher says: “What can you now explain that you could not explain at the start?”
Required homework: complete the six foundation practice items and correct one error.
Optional homework: attempt an improve or challenge item and explain your method.

HOME PRACTICE
# Understanding equivalent fractions
Grade 4 · Mathematics · Advanced
Curriculum: School curriculum · review required
3 sessions × 60 minutes · Focus: Whole-child learning

Curated, deterministic teaching template. Adapt to your class and verify local curriculum alignment.

10 items · 6 foundation (60%) / 3 improve (30%) / 1 challenge (10%). Suggested time: 20–30 minutes, not part of the 60-minute sessions.

## Foundation / required
1. Complete 1/2 = ?/4.
2. Complete 2/3 = ?/6.
3. Complete 3/5 = ?/10.
4. Simplify 2/4.
5. Simplify 4/8.
6. Is 3/4 = 6/8? Explain.

## Improve / optional
7. Show 2/5 = 4/10 with a diagram.
8. Explain the error: 1/3 = 1/6.
9. Find two fractions equivalent to 3/4.

## Challenge / optional
10. Explain why multiplying both parts by 3 preserves a fraction's value.

## Reference answers & reasoning
Check after attempting the questions. Open-ended answers may vary; reasoning must be accurate.
1. 2/4; both parts ×2.
2. 4/6; both parts ×2.
3. 6/10; both parts ×2.
4. 1/2; both parts ÷2.
5. 1/2; both parts ÷4.
6. Yes; both parts ×2.
7. Shade 2 of 5 equal sections, then split each into 2; 4 of 10 are shaded.
8. The denominator doubled without doubling the numerator; 1/6 is half of 1/3.
9. 6/8 and 9/12; scale both parts equally.
10. Each original piece is split into 3 equal pieces, so the total and selected piece counts both triple without changing the amount.
